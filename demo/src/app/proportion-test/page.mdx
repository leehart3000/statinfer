## When to use a proportion test

A proportion test compares **how often something happens**, when each try either succeeds or doesn't, such as a coin landing heads, a visitor buying something, or a part passing inspection. There are two kinds:

- **One sample:** is a proportion different from a target value? For example, is a coin that landed heads 58 times in 100 flips a fair coin, with a proportion of 0.5?
- **Two samples:** do two groups have different proportions? For example, did 45 of 120 visitors buy after seeing one design, compared with 30 of 110 after seeing another?

statinfer's proportion test is a **z-test**, which relies on an approximation that works well when the counts are reasonably large. A common rule of thumb is at least 10 successes and 10 failures in each group. When comparing two groups with smaller counts, Fisher's exact test is more reliable. The confidence interval is the simple "Wald" interval, which can be inaccurate for small samples, or for proportions close to 0 or 1.

### The raw result

`proportionTest` returns a plain JavaScript object, which your code can use directly. For example, `result.pValue` is the p-value. Below, the same object is shown as **JSON** (JavaScript Object Notation), a standard text format for data, which you can save or send to other programs. For one-sided tests, the confidence interval stops at a natural limit rather than at infinity: 0 or 1 for a proportion, and −1 or 1 for a difference between two proportions.
