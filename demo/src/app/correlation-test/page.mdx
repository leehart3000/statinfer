## When to use a correlation test

A correlation test asks whether **two measurements of the same things move together**. For example: do taller people tend to weigh more, or do students who study longer tend to score higher? Each x value must be paired with the y value in the same position.

The result, the **correlation**, runs from −1 to 1. A value near 1 means y rises steadily as x rises, near −1 means y falls steadily, and near 0 means no consistent relationship. The test asks whether the correlation in your data is strong enough to be more than chance. There are two methods:

- **Pearson** measures how closely the points follow a **straight line**. It's the usual choice, but a single extreme value can distort it.
- **Spearman** first converts the values to ranks (1st, 2nd, 3rd…), then measures how consistently y rises or falls with x, even along a curve. That makes it robust to outliers.

Correlation doesn't show that one thing *causes* the other: both might be driven by something else. A correlation test also needs at least three pairs, and more pairs make the result far more reliable.
